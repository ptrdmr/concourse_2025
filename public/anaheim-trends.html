Population & Demographics

Anaheim grew rapidly from 1980 to 2000 (+49.6%), then slowed. The 2020 Census counted 346,824 residents; the most recent ACS 1-year estimate (2024) is 344,579—essentially flat. The city is majority Hispanic/Latino (53.2%) with a significant Asian population (18.0%).

  • Average household size: 3.1 persons (2024 ACS 1-year estimate).

  • Growth has shifted from rapid expansion to a mature, stable population base.

Cost of Living & Housing

Anaheim's overall cost of living index is 164.9 (U.S. average = 100), driven primarily by housing costs. Median owner-occupied home value (ACS) is $831,200; Zillow's typical home value index (ZHVI) was $953,649 as of May 2026—a modeled market estimate, not a closed-sale median.

Housing affordability signals
  • ACS median gross rent ($2,175) consumes ~27% of median household income ($95,227).
  • Zillow typical home value (~$954K) is roughly 10× median household income.
  • Home values rose ~98% from Dec 2015 ($479K) to Dec 2024 ($947K), with a slight softening in 2025 before rebounding in 2026.

Tourism Indicators

  • 26 million visitors to Anaheim in 2024, up from 25.8M in 2023 (Visit Anaheim).
  • $6.6 billion in visitor spending (2024).
  • Disneyland Resort attendance: ~27.4 million (2024, TEA/AECOM); Disney CEO cited "almost 30 million" for 2025.
  • Anaheim Convention Center: 69 conventions, ~1M attendees, $1.2B economic impact (2023).
  • Resort-area hotel occupancy: 75.4% (FY 2023/24); average daily rate ~$237 (2023).
  • Disney's tax contribution to City of Anaheim: $279 million (FY 2023, Oxford Economics).
Business formation

California new business applications (EIN filings) remain elevated post-pandemic: ~558,612 in 2023 vs. ~363,771 in 2019 (+54%). 2024 cooled to ~517,133 but remains well above pre-pandemic norms (Census Business Formation Statistics).

Other Trends Worth Knowing

Development

ocVIBE — $4B Mixed-Use District

92–100 acres around Honda Center and ARTIC: 2,000+ apartments, two hotels, 35+ restaurants, entertainment venues. Phase I opening 2026–2027; much of project targets 2028 Olympics.

Development

DisneylandForward — $1.9B+ Expansion

City Council approved May 2024. Rezones ~490 acres of Disney-owned land for flexible theme park, hotel, and entertainment uses. Minimum $1.9B investment over 10 years; agreement extends through 2064.

Events

2028 Olympics Host Venue

Honda Center will host indoor volleyball. Visit Anaheim targets 30M annual visitors by 2028. U.S. DOT granted $5M for a pedestrian bridge connecting ARTIC to Honda Center.

Housing Policy

Inclusionary Housing Ordinance

Adopted Oct 2024. New rental projects with 35+ units must dedicate 10% to low/very-low income households or pay in-lieu fee. Over 30,000 households on Section 8 waitlist.

Infrastructure

ARTIC Transit Hub

Major renovations underway as part of ocVIBE. Plans include a fourth rail line for future California High-Speed Rail and an elevated pedestrian bridge to Honda Center.

Workforce

Tight Labor Market

OC unemployment ~3.7% (below state and national averages). Disneyland union wages reaching $26/hr by 2026. Wage pressure and talent competition remain key constraints for local employers.

Notes for Local Business Owners

  • Tourism is the economic engine. With 26M visitors and a path to 30M by 2028, businesses serving visitor spending (food, retail, entertainment, services) have a structurally expanding customer base.
  • Healthcare is the fastest-growing employment sector in the metro (+15.5% from 2022 to 2025), creating demand for ancillary services.
  • $14B+ in committed development (ocVIBE, DisneylandForward, residential construction) will bring construction workers, then permanent employees and residents—each wave creating new demand.
  • Housing costs remain the binding constraint. High home values and rents limit workforce availability; the inclusionary ordinance and new apartment supply are responses, but demand outstrips supply.
  • 2028 Olympics will be a one-time demand shock. Infrastructure being built now will serve the city for decades beyond the Games.

Sources & Data Caveats

This report synthesizes research from three parallel analyses (demographics, cost of living/housing, economy/business). Where sources conflict, decennial Census counts and ACS official estimates are preferred over third-party aggregators. Zillow ZHVI/ZORI values are modeled market indices, not official medians. Cost-of-living indexes from BestPlaces and RentCafe are proprietary models, not BLS Consumer Price Index data. Employment figures cover the Orange County metropolitan division, not Anaheim city alone.
